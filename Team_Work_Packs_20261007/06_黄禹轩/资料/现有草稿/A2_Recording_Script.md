# U Use | A2 Group Pitch Video

Target duration: 4:50, including pauses and transitions. Official requirement: 3-6 minutes. Deadline: 18 December 2026. The handbook does not specify a clock time. Check the separate assessment brief and LMO.

This is a complete recording script, not a completed MP4. Proposed speakers require team agreement. Research and product facts must be refreshed on the recording date. Synthetic demo accounts and listings must stay visibly labelled. Never show a real email, timetable, location, token or private chat.

## 0:00-0:40 | 郭宇璇（Rita） | Problem

**Shot:** Presenter, then an actual date-filtered product screen. Caption: “Local prototype. Synthetic demo data.”

**Narration:**

Hello, we are the U Use team. Our product helps students at SIP and XEC access an item when they need it. A short project, an event or a trip may require something a student does not want to own permanently. Finding an item is only part of the task. The two people still need compatible dates, an agreed meeting place and clear terms. That coordination problem is our starting hypothesis. We have built a working local prototype, and we are preparing to test whether it makes a real transaction easier.

## 0:40-1:20 | 毛相宜 | Product positioning

**Shot:** Date filters and a listing, followed by the needs-board tab. Do not stage a fake testimonial.

**Narration:**

U Use supports borrowing, renting, buying and swapping. Two features give our proposal a practical focus. First, a student can search for dates when the item is actually needed. Second, the needs board lets a student describe a specific request so an owner can offer a suitable item. We made the map optional because a transaction should not require public location sharing. Our differentiation is a hypothesis about easier coordination. We do not claim that existing marketplaces lack every one of these features, or that students have already validated our approach.

## 1:20-2:05 | 汪睿 | Demonstration

**Shot sequence:** Choose a campus and dates. Open a synthetic listing. Show availability and total consideration. Open a synthetic request and eligible response. Cut to reviewed timetable and booking recommendation.

**Narration:**

Here is the actual prototype, using synthetic data. I choose XEC and the dates I need, then review an item and its terms. If I cannot find suitable supply, I can post a dated request on the needs board. An owner can respond with their own eligible item. For the meeting, each participant may import their own timetable from a screenshot or PDF, or enter classes manually. The user corrects the extracted details before enabling matching. The application suggests a compatible time, but the participants still agree the place. It never assumes that all students share one timetable.

## 2:05-2:40 | 李元龙 | Alternatives

**Shot:** Editable comparison slide with official source URLs in notes and a small on-screen source label.

**Narration:**

Students already have alternatives. Goofish provides a general second-hand marketplace. Renrenzu presents rental listings, while Aihuishou supports recycling and resale. Borrowing from friends is also a meaningful substitute. Our comparison asks how quickly a student can find a suitable item and agree its use for specific dates. We will test the same task across alternatives. A missing homepage detail does not prove that a competitor lacks a feature. The opportunity is a focused campus service, and its value still needs evidence.

## 2:40-3:25 | 汤鋆秋 | Market and economics

**Shot:** Scenario workbook, showing inputs and resulting GMV. Caption: “Four-week planning scenario, not observed results.”

**Narration:**

XJTLU reports more than twenty-six thousand students, but that institutional total is not our reachable market. Our base planning case invites one hundred and twenty students through a permitted channel. With assumed activation of fifteen percent and completion of forty percent, we expect around seven trades in four weeks. At thirty yuan per paid trade, expected GMV is two hundred and sixteen yuan. Refundable deposits do not count as revenue. The pilot charges no platform fee and assumes one hundred and eighty yuan of cash costs. These economics support a cautious learning pilot, not broad paid marketing.

## 3:25-4:05 | 黄俊杰 | Validation

**Shot:** Research instrument and anonymised record structure, with status “Planned” rather than fabricated populated responses.

**Narration:**

Our next evidence comes from real participants. We will first ask about a recent borrowing or buying incident, then observe a task without leading the participant. The first round plans three to five interactions, with consent and anonymised notes. We will record task completion, confusion and matching failures. A small convenience sample can reveal usability problems but cannot prove campus-wide demand. We will also test different timetable formats and the option to proceed without GPS. Technical tests already exercise the workflow, but we keep that evidence separate from customer research.

## 4:05-4:50 | 黄禹轩 | Feasibility and close

**Shot:** Verification report, then team names and the request for five volunteers. Finish with an actual product screen.

**Narration:**

The prototype includes student-email verification, private schedules, ownership checks, booking validation and a dispute path. Payments remain off-platform. We do not offer insured trades or escrow. Public deployment still requires production email, HTTPS, database acceptance, a tested backup restore and an identified moderator. Our evidence register separates sources, assumptions, technical tests and participant records. We are asking for five volunteers and a permitted recruitment channel. The goal is a dependable first transaction. If users cannot find useful supply or the team cannot operate safely, we will narrow the service instead of claiming growth that the evidence does not support. Thank you.

## Recording Checklist

1. Confirm the seven speakers and rehearse with a stopwatch. Aim for 4:30-5:20, leaving room within the official 3-6 minute limit. The timing allocations are targets, not measured performance.
2. Update the narration if real research or deployment has happened. Replace planned wording only when a dated raw record supports it. State participant counts precisely.
3. Use isolated demo accounts. Remove emails, OTPs, student identifiers, uploaded originals, private conversations and identifiable locations from every shot.
4. Record the workflow in advance so loading or local OCR delays do not consume the pitch. Keep fallback screenshots from the same verified build. Avoid cutting footage in a way that implies an unimplemented action worked.
5. Use landscape 16:9, clear audio and legible captions. Export MP4 using the actual assessment brief's codec, size and upload instructions. Do not invent an official resolution or file-size limit.
6. Watch the complete exported video, check duration and audio, open the uploaded copy and retain the required submission receipt. A script or slide deck does not satisfy A2.
