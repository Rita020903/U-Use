## Reflection Focus: Traceability and team organisation

An evidence register allows the team to connect a claim to a source, an assumption or an executed test. Technical test results cannot replace participant evidence. Version histories need an identifiable owner and date.

## Evidence You Must Identify

Identify a record you actually maintained and a coordination problem you resolved. Explain how your system changed a team decision, with the underlying file or meeting record.

For every contribution claim, record date, exact task, personal action, evidence path, outcome and limitation. Do not claim teammates' or AI-generated work as your own. Proposed roles, synthetic fixtures and planned research do not prove completed work.

## Analytical Prompts (not required section headings)

1. Context and responsibilities: explain your actual role and the boundary between your work and the team's work.
2. A consequential decision: analyse one specific incident using its evidence, the alternative and your reasoning. Include what you initially misunderstood.
3. Collaboration: describe a real disagreement, dependency or feedback exchange. Explain your personal response and its effect.
4. Learning: connect your incident to a relevant course concept and source. Explain why the concept helps, and where it fails to explain the situation.
5. Adaptation and next steps: state what changed, what you would do differently and how you would assess improvement.

Use the four official themes: learning/skills; team dynamics including a tension/challenge; entrepreneurial mindset/identity using theory; future actions. These prompts support analysis, not a five-section substitute. Use a recognised reflective model. Mentors must not read, edit or comment on the reflection prose.

## Analytical Paragraph You Can Build On

An evidence register allows the team to connect a claim to a source, an assumption or an executed test. Technical test results cannot replace participant evidence. Version histories need an identifiable owner and date. A useful reflection therefore connects a specific personal action to a recorded result and critically examines another plausible explanation. For example, a clearer demo may improve communication without changing demand, and a passing test may establish a technical condition without establishing a viable business. The individual's reflection should explain that distinction through their own authentic experience.
